SCANNING GUIDE

Make a clean PDF from paper using your phone

A phone camera can be a practical document scanner when you need to turn notes, forms, receipts or printed pages into a single PDF. The important part is not only taking the photo: a useful scan also needs readable text, sensible cropping, correct orientation and consistent page order. Kitlune Scan to PDF is designed for that simple workflow in the browser. It does not send your selected photos to a Kitlune application server.

How to use Scan to PDF

  1. Tap Scan with camera on a phone to open the camera, or choose Add from gallery for photos you already took.
  2. Add up to 30 pages. Tap a thumbnail to edit that page. Move pages earlier or later if they are in the wrong order, and delete accidental shots.
  3. Use the four crop handles to remove desk space around the document. Rotate a sideways page before making the PDF.
  4. Try Document (clear) when the paper has uneven lighting or weak contrast. Original keeps the photo appearance, Grayscale removes colour, and B&W makes a strong two-tone version.
  5. Choose A4 for conventional document pages or Fit to image when you want the PDF page to follow each scan's proportions. Then create and download the PDF.

Why the Document (clear) filter can help

Phone photos often have a bright area near a lamp and darker areas near a hand, page fold or edge. A simple global brightness adjustment cannot correct both areas well. The document filter estimates the broad lighting across the page from a heavily blurred copy, normalizes the original against that background, and then stretches the tones so pale paper moves toward white while darker writing remains visible. This is followed by restrained contrast enhancement. The goal is the kind of cleaner document appearance people expect from a scanner, while avoiding a claim that every difficult photo can be repaired.

The B&W option goes further by turning the normalized result into dark and light pixels. It can make printed text very crisp, but it may remove pencil marks, faint handwriting, stamps, coloured highlights or subtle diagrams. Compare the preview before choosing it.

Tips for better document photos

Start with the best source photo you can. Put the page on a flat surface and hold the phone as parallel to the paper as possible. Fill most of the frame without cutting off corners. Use bright, even light from the side or from more than one direction. Avoid placing your hand or phone between the main light and the page because that creates a strong shadow. If a glossy page reflects a lamp, move the light or slightly change the phone angle rather than relying on software to remove glare.

Small handwriting needs resolution. The tool keeps a useful working resolution and only downsizes unusually large phone photos to reduce memory pressure. Even so, a sharp close photo will usually preserve text better than a distant photo that must be enlarged later.

Common mistakes

Do not crop so tightly that signatures, page numbers or handwritten notes disappear. Check that every page is upright. If you photographed a two-sided document, make sure both sides are included and ordered correctly. Do not assume a strong black-and-white filter is always better: faint blue ink or pencil can vanish. Also remember that this tool creates an image-based PDF. It does not perform OCR, so words in the finished PDF are not automatically selectable or searchable.

Worked example

Imagine you have four handwritten class notes. Photograph each page in order, then select the first thumbnail. Crop away the table around the notebook and choose Document (clear) to reduce the uneven brightness near the page edge. Repeat for the other pages, rotate one page that was captured sideways, and use the move buttons if pages three and four were added in the wrong order. Choose A4 and Better quality, create the PDF, download it, and open it once to confirm all four pages are readable before sharing it.

Limitations

This first version uses manual cropping rather than automatic document-edge detection, so you choose the page boundary yourself. It cannot recover text that is out of focus, hidden by glare or clipped outside the original photograph. Lighting normalization can improve many ordinary paper photos but may produce uneven results on coloured paper, photographs, textured backgrounds or pages with large illustrations. Processing many high-resolution pages can also use significant memory on older phones; if the browser struggles, use fewer pages at a time.

Privacy

Your selected images and generated PDF are processed in your browser. They are not uploaded to a Kitlune application backend by this tool. The page loads the pdf-lib JavaScript library from the public jsDelivr CDN when you use this page, so your browser makes a normal network request to that CDN for the library itself. The selected document images are not sent to jsDelivr by the scanning code.

Frequently asked questions

Does Scan to PDF automatically find the paper edges?

No. Version one uses touch-friendly manual crop handles so you remain in control of the boundary.

Which filter should I use?

Start with Document (clear) for ordinary paper. Use Original when colour matters, Grayscale for a neutral scan, or B&W for strong dark text on light paper.

Can I scan more than one page?

Yes. You can add and arrange up to 30 pages before creating one PDF.

Are my document photos uploaded?

No. The scanning and PDF creation are designed to happen locally in your browser.

Will the PDF text be searchable?

No. This tool creates an image-based PDF and does not run OCR.
